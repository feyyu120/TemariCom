
# Research

## 1. Student Research

To understand how students currently discover learning resources,
internships, scholarships, tutors, campus information, and other
opportunities, we asked students on campus.

### Research Question

How do students currently discover useful learning resources,
opportunities, tutors, and campus-related information?

### Method

We asked students about:

- Where they usually find learning materials
- How they discover internships and scholarships
- How they find tutors
- How they receive university announcements
- Whether they use multiple platforms or sources
- What difficulties they experience when searching for these resources

### Observations

- Students reported getting learning materials from previous Telegram
groups and from teachers who send materials through Telegram.

- Students reported finding internships, scholarships, and other
opportunities through Telegram groups and social media.

- Students reported finding tutors through Telegram groups and online
platforms.

- Students reported receiving university announcements through
Telegram groups and official social media accounts.

- Students reported using multiple platforms and sources to find
information.

- Students reported that searching for information can require
visiting other websites. They also described distractions on online
platforms, including advertisements and unrelated videos. These
distractions can cause students to lose focus on what they originally
intended to search for.

## 2. Academic Research

We searched ScholarXIV for academic research related to:

- student information-seeking behavior
- higher education information access
- student career information seeking
- internship and scholarship awareness
- digital learning resources
- student engagement with digital platforms

Several papers related to general information seeking and student
experiences were found. However, the initial searches did not identify
a paper that was directly relevant enough to the specific problem of
students finding internships, scholarships, learning resources, tutors,
and other opportunities across multiple platforms.

We therefore did not include unrelated papers as evidence for this
specific problem.

### Relevant Papers

No directly relevant paper was identified on ScholarXIV during our
initial academic search.

The search returned papers related to general information seeking,
including research on information seeking among international students
and research on career confidence among IT students. However, these
papers did not directly investigate how university students discover
internships, scholarships, tutors, learning materials, and other
opportunities across different platforms.
Because of this, these papers were not treated as direct evidence for
the TemariCom problem.

---

## 3. Research Conclusion

The student interviews provide direct evidence about the experiences
of the students we asked.

The students reported using multiple sources, especially Telegram
groups, social media, online platforms, teachers, and official social
media accounts to find learning materials, opportunities, tutors, and
university announcements.

The students also reported difficulties caused by having to search
across different websites and platforms. They described distractions
such as advertisements and unrelated videos, which can make it harder
to stay focused on the information they originally intended to find.

The ScholarXIV search provided some research related to information
seeking, but no directly relevant paper was identified for the specific
TemariCom problem during our initial search. Therefore, we avoided
using unrelated academic papers to support the problem.

Together, the student research and academic search helped us understand
the information-seeking challenges students experience and informed the
problem definition and features considered for TemariCom.
